Happy November, Insiders!

The holidays usually slow the market down, but this year looks different. Anthropic went public on November 19, and the buyers it could bring are real new competition for anyone already looking in San Francisco. Buyers who have been active are motivated to get in before that wave arrives. Sellers who list now, instead of waiting for what could be a strong 2027 spring season, have their own reasons, but they share one goal: to be under contract before year end. For buyers and sellers still active, there's a clear, short window of opportunity.

Personally, it's been a busy and fruitful stretch. There's a saying among REALTORS® that if you want business, go on vacation (and lo and behold!). 401 Crescent Ct #4101 made its market debut last Thursday, and we held our first open houses this past weekend. You can see the details at the listing website.

There's real opportunity here. The home is owner occupied today and will be delivered vacant. Owner-occupied homes can be a great opportunity for buyers, because showing and open house windows are harder to schedule. Fewer buyers and agents see the property, which means fewer offers come in. Staging is harder too. A vacant home gives stagers full creative freedom, and buyers like a furnished blank canvas where they can picture their own things in every room. In our case, showings are easy because the sellers are in the middle of moving. I might be biased, but the photos will back me up, so please come see it or share it with those who may be interested!

This Month's Numbers

San Francisco Isn't Taking a Holiday

Prices hit a three-year high in October, competition stayed fierce, and the luxury market is on a record pace. The market has not heard that it is supposed to slow down.

The median single-family home sold for $2.5 million in October, up 25.1% from a year ago and the highest monthly median in the three years of data I pulled. It took a median of just 12 days to go into contract. The median condo price was $1.35 million, up 10.8% year over year, and condos went into contract in a median of 14 days. A year ago that number was 26, and in January it was 76.

Inventory is still the story. Single-family listings slipped from 172 in September to 161, and condos edged up from 392 to 405. That works out to 1.0 months of supply for houses and 2.2 for condos, far below the four to six months that mark a balanced market.

Single-family homes sold for an average of 129% of their original list price in October. A year ago, that number was 115%.

The competition number is the one everyone will quote, and last month's Money Lesson explains why the list price is only the starting point in San Francisco. Condos tell a quieter version of the same story at 111.5% of original list price, up from 102.0% a year ago.

The Charts

What the Data Says

SF Median Sale Price — Single-Family Homes

What it means: After a summer dip to $1.908M in August, the median climbed to $2.2M in September and $2.5M in October, the highest month in the three years charted. That is $502,000 above October 2025. Medians swing with the mix of homes that sell, so treat one month as a snapshot. The direction is what matters.

SF Median Sale Price — Condos

What it means: The median condo price was $1.35M in October, up 10.8% from $1.218M a year ago. Since February the monthly median has stayed between $1.21M and $1.43M. Condos are holding their ground while single-family homes pull away.

Sale Price vs. Original List Price — SFH vs. Condos

What it means: Single-family homes averaged 129.2% of original list price in October, down from 133.0% in September and well above 115.4% a year ago. Condos averaged 111.5%, up from 110.6% in September and 102.0% a year ago.

Months of Supply — SFH vs. Condos

What it means: Single-family homes have 1.0 months of supply and condos have 2.2. A balanced market is generally considered four to six months. Condo buyers have about twice the selection house hunters do, and both sit far below balance. A year ago the figures were 1.2 and 2.8.

Active Inventory — SFH vs. Condos

What it means: There were 161 active single-family listings and 405 active condo listings at the end of October. That is 16% fewer houses than a year ago and 14% fewer condos. Single-family inventory slipped from 172 in September while condos edged up from 392.

30-Year Fixed Mortgage Rate — Weekly

What it means: Rates climbed from 6.71% in early September to 7.40% in mid-October and have eased to 7.28% as of November 19. On a $1.2M loan, the move from 6.71% to 7.28% adds roughly $460 per month in principal and interest.

National Backdrop

The Bigger Picture

Most of the country is cooling. San Francisco is not. Here is the context for why both can be true.

Mortgage Rates

The 30-year fixed averaged 7.28% on November 19, up from 7.11% the week before and below last month's 7.40%. Jake Krimmel, a senior economist at Realtor.com, estimates that the climb from 6.6% in August to 7.4% in October cost buyers 7.2% of their purchasing power. Sources: Freddie Mac, Realtor.com.

Fewer Buyers, Longer Outlook

Mortgage purchase applications fell sharply as rates moved above 7% this fall. Fannie Mae, the Mortgage Bankers Association and Wells Fargo all expect rates to stay elevated through 2027, in a range of 6.9% to 7.5%. Buyers waiting for a big drop may be waiting a while. Source: Keeping Current Matters.

Sellers Outnumber Buyers

Redfin estimates there are about 60% more sellers than buyers nationwide, the widest gap in the decade it charts. During the 2020 to 2022 boom, buyers outnumbered sellers by a wide margin. That has fully reversed. Source: Redfin.

A Cooling National Market

Existing home sales fell to an annualized pace of 3.75 million in October, the lowest in more than a year. Inventory climbed to 1.59 million homes, the highest of 2026, even though new listings fell nearly 10%. The national median price slipped nearly 6% from $442,800 in June to $416,700 in October, and the country now has about 5.1 months of supply.

Price Cuts Offset Some of the Pain

Higher rates have cost the typical buyer about $22,000 in purchasing power, while the typical price reduction on a median-priced home is around $18,000. That offsets much of the loss nationally, but price reductions are far less predictable in competitive markets like San Francisco. Source: HousingWire.

The Money Lesson

What Anthropic's IPO Means for Buyers, and How Equity Can Fund an Offer

Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq on November 19. For employees holding equity, that raises a practical question: how do you turn shares into a competitive offer in San Francisco without giving up the position you have spent years building?

Selling is the simplest route, but it triggers taxes and gives up future upside. Some lenders offer another path. Vested shares can be pledged as collateral for a loan, which can fund a down payment or show cash on an offer while you keep the shares. The loan is usually repaid from future sales, bonus income or refinancing.

What to know before you plan around it

1. Unvested units generally cannot be borrowed against. Lenders look at what you own today, not what is scheduled to vest.

2. Your company's rules come first. Trading windows and lock-up periods can restrict what you are allowed to pledge or sell, so read your own plan before you talk to anyone.

3. Lenders usually lend a fraction of the share value. A falling stock price can also trigger a request for more collateral, and a large position in a single stock raises that risk.

Insider Spot

The Claypot House

1000 Clement St · Claypot Rice & Cantonese Soup

1 / 4

As the weather turns colder and the cravings turn cozier, the claypot rice at The Claypot House is high on my restaurant shortlist. The portions are generous. One order easily feeds two people with leftovers, or three moderately hungry people.

My favorite part is the black vinegar and jalapeño sauce you spoon over the rice. The classic Cantonese soup is a close second. It's light but packed with nourishing ingredients like carrots and tofu skin.

When I came with a party of three, we got a larger portion of soup and a complimentary serving of Chinese broccoli. I can't promise that's standard, but it's worth asking.

What I'd Order

Combo 1, with eel, chicken and Chinese sausage. The chicken is done so well it's velvety soft. Chicken and mushroom is my mom's favorite.
