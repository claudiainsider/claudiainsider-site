As of August 18, 1,347 single-family homes have sold in San Francisco this year. 89% of them went over asking at an average of 23% above list. The median list price was $923 per square foot; the median closed price was $1,161. That gap is a deliberate feature of the market, not a glitch.

What "Over Asking" Actually Means

"Sold 30% over asking" doesn't necessarily mean someone paid 30% more than the home was worth. In many cases, the home was intentionally listed below what comparable sales suggested it would command. The gap is a pricing strategy, not a mistake.

Take a home worth roughly $2.2 million. A seller could list at $1.895 million, $2.095 million, $2.195 million, or $2.295 million. Each price sends a different signal, attracts a different buyer pool, and can produce a genuinely different outcome. The highest list price doesn't always produce the highest sale price â sometimes pricing lower creates the competition that pushes the final number higher.

But that strategy isn't right for every property. A desirable single-family home with a deep buyer pool? Aggressive pricing can work beautifully. A condo, a luxury property, a tenant-occupied home, or something with a more limited audience? Completely different approach. There is no universal "San Francisco pricing strategy." It has to match the property, the neighborhood, the competition, and the moment.

For buyers, the flip side matters just as much: don't confuse asking price with affordability. A listing at $1.8 million might not actually be competing in the $1.8 million market. And a home sitting at $2.2 million for several weeks might have more negotiating room than the sticker suggests.

National Backdrop

What's Happening Beyond San Francisco

Mortgage Rates: Still Waiting

The 30-year fixed sits at 6.67% as of August 13 â slightly higher than a year ago. The 15-year is at 5.96%. Rates bottomed out in late February and have been climbing since.

What it means: If you've been waiting for a dramatic drop, you're still waiting. Forecasters project low-to-mid 6% through mid-2027.

Inflation: Depends Which Number You're Looking At

CPI showed prices rising just 0.1% in July with core inflation easing to 2.5% year over year. But the PCE index â the Fed's preferred measure â is moving the opposite direction and remains above the 2% target. Stubborn inflation makes it harder for mortgage rates to come down.

What it means: Fannie Mae, MBA, and Wells Fargo all project the 30-year fixed in the low-to-mid 6% range through mid-2027. Stability might be the better story here.

New Listings: Up Nationally, Down in the West

New listings rose 2.4% nationally, led by a 12.6% jump in the Northeast. The West? Down 0.8%. Our part of the country continues to be its own animal â and tight inventory here is a big part of why the overbidding numbers are what they are.

The Money Lesson

List Price Is a Strategy, Not a Valuation

141 homes sold $1M+ over asking this year. 1 in 10. Last year it was 1 in 109. The overbid headlines are real, but the lesson behind them matters more: in San Francisco, asking price is often a deliberate strategy, not a reflection of what the home is actually worth.

That distinction has never mattered more. Buyers need to understand what a home is likely to actually sell for before deciding whether to compete. Sellers need a pricing strategy built for their specific property, neighborhood, and buyer pool. And both need an agent who can tell the difference between an intentionally low list price and a genuine opportunity.

If you're on the sidelines, fall could be an interesting window: slightly more inventory now, and any meaningful rate decline would bring more competition, not less.
