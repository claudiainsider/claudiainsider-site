What is a TIC?

Two or more owners each hold a fractional interest in a single property. You own a share of the whole building and have exclusive rights to your specific unit — like a condo, but structured differently on paper.

Most SF TICs are SACOs (Space-Assignment Co-Ownerships): each owner gets exclusive use of a defined unit.

The TICA — Your Co-Ownership Rulebook

The TIC Agreement governs how the building runs. A strong TICA covers:

Space
Unit boundaries, parking, storage, shared areas
Finances
Monthly dues, reserves, payment formulas, default procedures
Governance
Decision-making (majority vs. unanimous), meetings, disputes
Rules
Pets, noise, renovations, rental restrictions, sale approvals
Legal
Insurance, lender protections, condo conversion provisions
A thin or outdated TICA isn't a dealbreaker — but it's a conversation you want early. Always have a TIC attorney review it.
Buyer Checklist
  • Individual or blanket financing?
  • TICA reviewed by a TIC-experienced attorney?
  • All owners current on dues and taxes?
  • Reserve fund in place?
  • Rental provisions checked? (SF rent control applies)
  • Condo conversion eligibility researched?
  • Unit boundaries, parking, storage clearly defined?
  • Co-owner financials evaluated?
  • Building maintenance history reviewed?
Why TICs Work in SF

SF's Victorian and Edwardian building stock is built for shared ownership. Local lenders, attorneys, and agents have decades of TIC-specific experience. For many buyers, a TIC is the way into a neighborhood they love at a price that makes sense.
