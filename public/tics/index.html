Understanding TICs

Half the Victorians you fall for on a Sunday walk are TICs. They're 10–20% below a comparable condo, they come with their own lenders and their own rulebook, and once you understand the structure they are a lot less mysterious than they sound. This is the version I'd explain to a friend.

Two or more owners each hold a fractional interest in one property. You get your own deed, your own loan, and exclusive rights to your unit. The lot is never subdivided, so there's one tax bill split between owners, and one agreement that runs the building.

Most SF TICs are space-assignment co-ownerships: the agreement assigns each owner a specific flat plus its parking, storage, and deck. Day to day it feels like a condo. On paper it's a partnership, which is why the paperwork matters more.

The TICA is the rulebook and the first document I read on any TIC listing. A thin or outdated one isn't a dealbreaker, but it's a conversation we have early, and a TIC attorney reviews it before you sign anything.
